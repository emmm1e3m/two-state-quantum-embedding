Reversal fraction $\Delta/\eps$ for two explicit constructions at matched disturbance $\eps=P(0|1)$, where $\Delta=\eps-P(0|0)$ and $L_\eps=\log(1/\eps)$. Solid curves give attainable values for the tilted pure stationary state (circles) and the diagonal stationary state (squares). At $\eps=10^{-3}$ the diagonal construction gives the larger fraction ($0.205$ versus $0.192$). At smaller disturbances the tilted construction overtakes it. The dashed curves show the respective leading terms $4/(eL_\eps)$ and $64/(e^2 L_\eps^2)$. The upper axis gives the corresponding disturbance.}

\newcommand{\ResetConvergenceCaption}{%
Approach of the explicit constructions to their asymptotic reversal coefficients. Here $\Delta=\eps-P(0|0)$, $\eps=P(0|1)$, and $L_\eps=\log(1/\eps)$. (a) For the tilted pure stationary state, $\Delta L_\eps/\eps$ tends to $4/e$. (b) For the diagonal stationary state, $\Delta L_\eps^2/\eps$ tends to $64/e^2$. Solid curves evaluate the explicit solutions. Horizontal dashed lines mark the respective limits.}

\newcommand{\ResetTrajectoriesCaption}{%
Population reversal and remaining input distinguishability for the tilted pure stationary state (left) and the diagonal stationary state (right). Both constructions give $\eps_\star=P(0|1;t_{\rm r})=10^{-4}$ at readout time $t_{\rm r}=1$ (open circles). (a),(b) The two input-conditioned populations cross at $t_c$ (vertical dashed line) and then separate in reverse order. (c),(d) The population distance $D_{\rm pop}=|P(0|0;t)-P(0|1;t)|$ vanishes at the crossing (solid dot), while the trace distance $D_{\rm tr}=\tfrac12\|\rho_0(t)-\rho_1(t)\|_1$ remains positive. At that instant all input distinguishability is carried by the off-diagonal part of the state difference. The population distance subsequently revives while the trace distance continues to decrease. Both distances and both populations are normalized by $\eps_\star$. The lower panels use a linear distance scale up to $10^{-3}$ and a logarithmic scale above it. The curves evaluate the two-dimensional evolution of each finite realization.}
